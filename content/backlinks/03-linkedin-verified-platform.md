Every crypto-to-INR service in India calls itself trusted and verified. The
words cost nothing. What costs something — engineering, process, and
transparency — is making every claim independently checkable.

After watching how sellers actually get hurt in this market, I hold any
platform (including ones I use) to five checks:

1. Deposits verified on the public blockchain automatically — not by a
human reading wallet screenshots.
2. The rate locked in writing before any crypto moves — a quote that can
change after you commit is not a quote.
3. Real accounts and receipts — order history, verified email, and a
receipt carrying the on-chain transaction ID for every payout.
4. A stated answer to "whose money pays me?" — the platform's own
documented float, or anonymous third parties?
5. Indian rails treated as first-class — UPI/IMPS payouts that work at
2 a.m., IFSC validation, rupee amounts in lakh where they belong.

Any platform that passes all five can be tested in ten minutes with a small
order — and a platform that fails even one is asking you to trust
adjectives. I wrote up the full test, including
[what a verified Indian platform should actually mean](TARGET_URL), for
anyone who wants to run it on their current provider.

The best part of writing checks like these: they are vendor-neutral. Run
them on everyone — including us.
