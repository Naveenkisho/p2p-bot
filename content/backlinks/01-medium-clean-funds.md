Everyone selling USDT in India compares rates. Almost nobody asks the
question that actually decides whether the sale was worth it: whose rupees
am I about to receive?

Here is the uncomfortable mechanics of it. On open P2P marketplaces, anyone
with a bank account can buy your USDT. Fraud rings exploit exactly that —
they take money stolen from a phishing victim, buy crypto from an honest
seller, and disappear. Weeks later the victim files a complaint, the money
trail is followed, and every account it touched gets frozen. Including the
honest seller's salary account. Getting a freeze lifted can take months.

The fix is not "be careful" — you cannot see a payment's history in your
banking app. ₹50,000 from a legitimate buyer and ₹50,000 from a fraud haul
look identical when they land. The fix is structural: sell to a counterparty
whose money has a boring, documented history. A desk that pays from its own
operating float, verifies your deposit on the public blockchain, and gives
you a receipt with the transaction ID has removed the stranger from the
transaction entirely.

That is the difference between chasing half a rupee on the rate and
protecting the account your salary lands in. I wrote a longer breakdown of
how to [sell USDT and get clean funds](TARGET_URL) — including a five-point
checklist you can run on any platform before you accept a single rupee.

Rate-shopping is fine. Just ask the money question first.
