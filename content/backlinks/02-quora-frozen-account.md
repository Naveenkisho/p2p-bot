Short answer: it is almost never because selling USDT is illegal — it is
because of whose money paid you.

When you sell on a P2P marketplace, a stranger transfers rupees to your
bank. If that stranger paid you with money stolen from someone else (a very
common fraud pattern), the victim eventually files a cyber complaint. Banks
then trace the stolen amount and freeze every account it passed through —
including yours, even though you sold in good faith and delivered the
crypto honestly.

Three practical protections, from most to least effective:

1. Sell to a single known counterparty (an OTC desk that pays from its own
float) instead of a rotating cast of marketplace strangers — this removes
the risk at the source rather than managing it.
2. Keep evidence for every trade: order ID, on-chain transaction hash, the
rate, and who paid you. Screenshots of a chat are weak; a receipt with the
blockchain TXID is strong.
3. Never let "great rate" override an odd payment pattern — payments from a
different name than the buyer, split payments, or "I'll pay from my
friend's account" are the classic tells.

checklist. Worth ten minutes before your next sale, whatever platform you
use.
