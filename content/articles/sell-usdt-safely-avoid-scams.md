USDT itself is simple. The danger in selling it is entirely about **who you
sell to** and **what process you follow**. These are the scams that actually
catch sellers in India, and the process that defeats every one of them.

## The five scams that do the damage

### 1. The fake payment screenshot

You send USDT first; the "buyer" sends a doctored UPI success screenshot.
By the time you check your actual bank balance, the USDT is gone and
irreversible. Defence: never treat a screenshot as payment. Money is in your
account when your **bank** says so, nowhere else.

### 2. The reversed payment

The buyer pays you from a compromised account or raises a dispute after
receiving your USDT. The INR leaves your account weeks later; the crypto
does not come back. Defence: sell to a counterparty whose funds are
verifiably clean — this is exactly what the
[clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee) exists for.

### 3. The address swap

Malware or a fake support agent swaps the deposit address in your clipboard
or chat. USDT lands in a stranger's wallet. Defence: copy the address only
from your live order page, verify the first and last characters after
pasting, and never accept an address sent in a chat message.

### 4. The wrong-network "helper"

Someone convinces you to send BEP20 USDT to a TRC20 address "because it's
faster". The transfer vanishes into an address nobody controls. Defence:
pick the network on the order form and match it exactly in your wallet at
withdrawal time.

### 5. The rate that moves after you send

"Market moved, boss — new rate." Your USDT is already in their wallet, so
the renegotiation only goes one way. Defence: the rate must be locked, in
writing, on a page you can revisit, **before** anything is sent. On this
desk the rate is written into your order the moment you
[place it](/sell).

## The process that beats all five at once

Notice the pattern: every scam exploits either trust in a human, or a
missing verification step. The fix is a process where no step depends on
trusting a person:

- The rate is locked into the order automatically — no renegotiation is possible.
- The deposit is verified **on the blockchain itself** by a scanner matching your exact unique amount — no screenshots in either direction, as explained in the [pricing and transaction guide](/legal/transactions).
- The payout comes from verified clean sources, so there is nothing to reverse and no dirty sender in the chain.
- Every completed order gets a proof card, and your [order page](/my) shows the live status and the transaction hash on a public explorer at every step.

## Red flags that end a deal immediately

- Any request to send USDT before the rate is fixed in writing
- An address delivered in chat instead of on an order page
- Pressure to hurry ("rate valid 2 minutes only")
- A buyer who cannot say where their INR comes from
- Payout offered to someone else's bank account

## If something already went wrong

Speed matters more than blame. If you sent to a wrong or suspicious party,
immediately save every artifact — the transaction hash, addresses, chat
history, the page you were shown — before anything disappears. If money
left your bank under pressure, call your bank's fraud line the same day;
the earlier a dispute is raised, the more options exist. And if the issue
is simply "I paid but nothing credited" on a legitimate platform, use its
evidence-based recovery: on this desk you paste your transaction hash and
the chain itself is checked — no arguing, just verification. Honest
platforms want your evidence; scam operations want your patience. How each
side reacts to a problem tells you which one you are dealing with.
