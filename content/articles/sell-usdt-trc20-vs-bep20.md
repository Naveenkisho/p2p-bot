The USDT is identical; the road it travels is not. When you sell, your
exchange or wallet asks which network to withdraw on — usually **TRC-20
(TRON)** or **BEP-20 (BSC)** — and the choice affects your fee, your speed,
and the one mistake that can genuinely cost money. Here is the honest
comparison, from a desk that accepts both.

## TRC-20 (TRON) — the workhorse

Most USDT in Asia moves on TRON. Addresses start with **T**. Transfers
confirm in about a minute and exchange withdrawal fees are usually around
1 USDT. Every exchange and every wallet supports it — if you want the
zero-surprises option, this is it.

## BEP-20 (BSC) — the fast lane

BEP-20 runs on BNB Smart Chain. Addresses start with **0x**. Blocks land
every ~3 seconds, so a transfer is typically final in **15–25 seconds** —
noticeably quicker than TRON — and withdrawal fees are often lower.
On this desk BEP-20 deposits are detected the moment they are mined and
credited seconds later; if you like watching the
[order page](/learn/sell-usdt-instantly) flip to "detected" almost as soon
as your exchange says "withdrawal completed", pick BEP-20.

## The one rule that protects your money

**The network you withdraw on must match the network of the deposit
address.** A TRON address cannot receive a BEP-20 transfer and vice versa.
The good news: wallets and exchanges enforce the address format — a T…
address won't validate as BEP-20 — so the classic disaster is rare. The
realistic mistake is subtler: picking TRC-20 on your exchange while your
order shows a 0x address, then wondering why nothing arrives. Slow down at
one moment only: **check the first characters of the address against the
network you selected.** T… means TRON; 0x… means BSC.

On this desk you choose the network when you
[open the order](/sell), and the page then shows one address, one QR and
one amount for exactly that network — there is nothing to mix up. If you
ever do send on the wrong network from an exchange, don't panic and don't
guess: paste your transaction hash on the order page and the desk checks
the actual chain — [how that verification works](/learn/verified-indian-usdt-platform).

## Fees and timing, side by side

- **Confirmation:** BEP-20 ~15–25 s · TRC-20 ~60 s.
- **Typical exchange withdrawal fee:** BEP-20 often lower; both usually
  under a dollar's worth.
- **Address format:** TRC-20 starts with T · BEP-20 starts with 0x.
- **Rate you're paid:** identical — the network changes the road, never
  the price. Your INR quote is locked before you send either way.

## So which should you pick?

If your exchange offers both at similar fees: **BEP-20 for speed,
TRC-20 for habit** — both end the same way, with
[clean INR in your bank](/learn/sell-usdt-clean-funds) and a receipt
carrying the on-chain transaction ID. What actually matters is the address
check above and sending the exact amount your order shows, decimals and
all. Get those two right and either network will feel instant.

## What about the other networks?

USDT also exists on Ethereum (ERC-20), Polygon, Solana and more. For
selling into INR they rarely make sense: ERC-20 withdrawal fees can eat
several dollars for no gain in speed or safety, and the less common
networks aren't supported by most Indian-facing buyers, this desk included.
Stick to the two rails everyone supports and fees stay negligible. One
more habit worth keeping regardless of network: after your exchange says
"withdrawal completed", copy the transaction hash it shows you. You will
almost never need it — detection is automatic — but if anything ever looks
stuck, that one string lets the desk check the actual chain in seconds and
settles any question with evidence instead of screenshots.
