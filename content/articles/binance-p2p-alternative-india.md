Binance P2P and similar marketplaces are how most Indians first sold USDT.
They work — millions of trades settle on them. But "it works" and "it is the
right tool for you" are different claims, and this comparison is honest
about both directions.

## What a P2P marketplace actually is

On Binance P2P (or any P2P board), the platform is a **noticeboard plus
escrow**. You pick a buyer from a list, the platform locks the buyer's USDT
escrow — wait, as a seller it locks **your** USDT — the buyer transfers INR
to your bank, you confirm receipt, and the platform releases your USDT to
them. The platform never touches the INR. That one fact drives every
difference below.

## Where the marketplace model is strong

- **Choice**: dozens of buyers competing on rate at any hour.
- **Escrow**: the crypto side is protected — a buyer cannot take USDT without you releasing it.
- **Scale**: deep liquidity for common amounts.

## Where the risk actually sits

The INR leg is a private transfer between two strangers' bank accounts. The
platform's escrow cannot see it, cannot verify its source, and cannot
reverse-proof it. So the seller carries:

- **Source risk** — you receive money from an account you know nothing about. If it traces back to fraud, your account is the one frozen: the full mechanics are in our [bank-freeze guide](/learn/avoid-bank-freeze-when-selling-usdt).
- **Confirmation games** — fake payment screenshots and "I paid, release now" pressure, covered in the [safety guide](/learn/sell-usdt-safely-avoid-scams).
- **Appeal time** — disputes are decided by platform support on screenshots, and your USDT stays locked while that plays out.

Marketplaces mitigate with buyer ratings and completion percentages. That
filters obvious bad actors; it cannot tell you whether this particular
payment's money is clean, because nobody on the platform knows.

## What an OTC desk changes

An OTC desk like this one is a **counterparty, not a noticeboard**. You sell
to the desk itself:

- One **locked rate** written into your order — no browsing, no negotiation, no rate games after you commit. Current numbers are on the [live rate grid](/#rates).
- The deposit is verified **on-chain automatically** by exact amount — no screenshots, no release button, no appeal theatre. See [how a sale runs end to end](/learn/how-to-sell-usdt-for-inr).
- The INR leg comes from the desk's own verified sources — market settlements, cash deposits, card settlements — backed by the [clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee). The source risk that P2P leaves with you is the exact thing the desk takes on.

The honest trade-off: you give up rate-shopping across many buyers, and you
are trusting one named counterparty instead of a platform's escrow. Judge
that counterparty the way this site's own guides tell you to judge anyone:
written rate lock, verifiable on-chain process, explained source of funds,
payout only to your own account.

## So which should you use?

- Selling small amounts occasionally and enjoy comparing offers → a P2P marketplace is fine; just follow the safety checklist rigorously, every time.
- Selling regularly, in serious amounts, or your bank account matters to your livelihood → the freeze risk on random buyers becomes the dominant cost, and a clean-funds OTC desk exists precisely to remove it.

Both tools are legitimate. Pick based on where you want the risk to sit —
with you, or with your counterparty. When you're ready to try the desk
model, [the sell form is here](/sell).

## Moving over without disruption

Switching routes doesn't need to be an event. Keep your exchange account —
it remains useful for buying USDT and for market data — and simply change
where you sell: withdraw to the desk's address instead of listing a P2P ad.
Your first desk sale should be deliberately small, treated as a test of the
five checks covered above; scale only after the rupees land as quoted.
Most sellers who switch report the same two differences first: nothing to
negotiate (the quote is the quote), and nothing to babysit (no buyer chat,
no appeal timers — the blockchain and the bank do the talking). The rate
conversation becomes a comparison of written quotes rather than a
marketplace gamble, which is exactly how selling should feel.
