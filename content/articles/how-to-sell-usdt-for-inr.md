If you hold USDT (Tether) and want rupees in your bank account, you have three
realistic routes: an Indian exchange, peer-to-peer (P2P) marketplaces, or an
OTC desk like this one. This guide walks through the OTC route step by step,
and is honest about when the other two make more sense.

## What selling USDT for INR actually involves

Every sale has the same three parts, whoever you sell to:

- **A rate** — how many rupees you get per USDT.
- **A transfer** — you send USDT on a blockchain network (usually TRC20 on TRON, sometimes BEP20 on BSC).
- **A payout** — the buyer sends INR to your bank by UPI, IMPS, or another method.

Everything that goes wrong in this market goes wrong in one of those three
parts: a rate that changes after you commit, a transfer to the wrong address
or network, or a payout that never comes — or comes from dirty money and
freezes your account. A good process closes all three gaps.

## Step 1: lock your rate before you send anything

Never send USDT to anyone on a "we'll see the rate later" basis. On this desk
the rate you see when you [place your order](/sell) is the rate you are paid
at — it is written into the order and cannot move afterwards. Check the live
rate grid on the home page first; each payout method (UPI, IMPS, CDM, cheque)
shows its own rate and its own minimum and maximum per order.

## Step 2: send the exact amount, on the right network

After you order, you get a deposit address, a QR code, and an **exact amount**
with unique paise. That exactness is not decoration — it is how the scanner
matches your deposit to your order automatically on the public blockchain, with
no screenshots and no "please confirm" messages. Two rules:

- Send **exactly** the amount shown, decimals included.
- Send on the **network you picked** — TRC20 USDT to a TRC20 address. Wallets show the network at withdrawal time; it must match.

If your wallet deducts its fee from the amount you enter, add the fee on top so
the exact amount arrives. The [transaction info page](/legal/transactions)
explains the unique-paise system in more detail.

## Step 3: get paid — and know where the money comes from

Once the deposit confirms on-chain (usually seconds), your order enters the
payout queue and the desk's admins pay your bank directly. The part most
sellers never think about: **whose money arrives in your account**. Payouts
here come only from verified clean sources — market and mutual-fund
settlements, cash deposits, card and gateway settlements — which is the whole
point of the [clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee). If you sell to a random P2P
buyer instead, you inherit whatever their money touched, and that is how bank
accounts end up frozen. Our guide to
[avoiding bank freezes](/learn/avoid-bank-freeze-when-selling-usdt) covers
this in depth.

## When an exchange or P2P marketplace is the better choice

Honesty over marketing: if you want to hold INR on the platform and trade
back and forth, an Indian exchange is built for that. If you want to shop
dozens of buyers and manage the risk yourself, P2P marketplaces exist — read
our [comparison with Binance P2P](/learn/binance-p2p-alternative-india)
before you decide. An OTC desk is the right tool when you want one fixed
rate, one transfer, and rupees in your own bank without negotiating with
strangers.

## Quick checklist before you sell

- Rate locked in writing (on your order page) before any transfer
- Address copied from the live order page, never from a chat message
- Exact amount, right network, fee added on top
- Payout to a bank account in **your own name**
- Seller explains where their INR comes from — if they can't, walk away
