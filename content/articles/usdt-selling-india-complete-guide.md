This is the zoomed-out guide: not one trade, but the whole landscape of
selling USDT (Tether) in India — what is legal, what is taxed, which network

## Is selling USDT legal in India?

Holding and selling crypto assets is **not illegal** in India. Crypto is not
legal tender, but it is a recognised, taxed asset class — the government
defined "virtual digital assets" (VDAs) in the Income-tax Act and taxes
dealing in them. What IS illegal is using crypto for unlawful purposes, and
what is risky is receiving tainted INR for it — a separate problem covered
in our [bank-freeze guide](/learn/avoid-bank-freeze-when-selling-usdt).

## The tax picture in plain words

Two mechanisms matter to a seller (rates as commonly applied to VDAs —
confirm current rules with a chartered accountant, this is not tax advice):

- **Tax on gains**: profits from transferring VDAs are taxed at a flat rate, without the usual deductions or loss set-offs that apply to other assets.
- **TDS on transfers**: a small percentage TDS applies on VDA transfers above thresholds, which buyers/platforms may be required to deduct.

The practical takeaway: keep a record of every sale — date, amount, rate,
and the transaction hash. On this desk that record builds itself: your
[order history](/my) keeps each order's exact amounts, rate, timestamps and
on-chain hash. Hand that to your CA at filing time.

## TRC20 vs BEP20 — which network to sell on

USDT exists on many blockchains; in India two dominate for transfers:

- **TRC20 (TRON)** — the default. Fast confirmation and very low transfer fees; every exchange and wallet supports it.
- **BEP20 (BSC)** — equally fast, useful when your USDT already sits on BSC (e.g. from certain wallets or DeFi).

The only rule that matters: the network you withdraw on must match the
network of the deposit address, exactly. Wrong-network transfers are the
most common self-inflicted loss in this market — the
[safety guide](/learn/sell-usdt-safely-avoid-scams) covers this and the
other classic mistakes.

## The three selling routes, honestly compared

- **Indian exchanges** — best when you want to trade repeatedly and hold INR balances on-platform. KYC, order books, withdrawal timelines.
- **P2P marketplaces** — best when you want to choose among many buyers and manage counterparty risk yourself. See the [Binance P2P comparison](/learn/binance-p2p-alternative-india) for what that risk management actually involves.
- **OTC desks** (this site) — best when you want one locked rate, one transfer, and INR in your own bank account, with the desk taking on the counterparty and fund-sourcing risk. How that works step by step: [the selling guide](/learn/how-to-sell-usdt-for-inr).

## What a professional setup looks like

Sellers who move USDT regularly converge on the same habits: rate locked in
writing before sending, exact-amount transfers on a verified address, payout
only to their own account, a written source-of-funds commitment from the
buyer (that is the [clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee) here), and complete
records for tax. None of it is complicated — it is just discipline, applied
every single time.

## A complete first-sale checklist

If this is your first time, run this list top to bottom and nothing will
surprise you: pick your route (exchange, P2P, or OTC) knowing the
trade-offs above; verify the platform before trusting it with size — a
small test order tells you more than any review; lock the rate in writing
before sending anything; check the address format matches the network you
picked on your exchange; send the exact amount your order shows, decimals
included; save the transaction hash your wallet or exchange displays; and
confirm the INR that lands matches the quote you locked. Seven steps,
five minutes, zero drama. Every one of them exists because someone,
somewhere, skipped it once — the guides linked through this page cover
what happened next.
