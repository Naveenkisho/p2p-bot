Search for a place to sell USDT in India and every result calls itself
"trusted" and "verified". The words are free. What is not free is the
machinery behind them — and that machinery is checkable. This guide lists the
five things that actually make a USDT-to-INR platform verified, and a
ten-minute test you can run on any desk before trusting it with a serious
amount, including this one.

## Verified by whom?

There is no government badge for OTC crypto desks in India, so "verified"
can only honestly mean one thing: **every claim the platform makes is
independently checkable by you.** The rate it promises is written into your
order. The deposit it says it received is visible on a public blockchain.
The payout it says it sent is in your bank statement. A platform is verified
when you never have to take its word for anything.

## The five checks that matter

**1. Your deposit is verified on-chain, automatically.** When you send USDT,
the platform should detect and confirm your exact transfer on the public
blockchain — not ask you for a screenshot. On this desk the order page
watches the blockchain live: it shows your transaction the moment it is
detected and credits it after network confirmations, with the transaction ID
displayed to you. How that flow works end to end:
[the step-by-step selling guide](/learn/how-to-sell-usdt-for-inr).

**2. The rate is locked in writing before you send.** A verified platform
quotes first and holds the quote. Your order here carries the exact rate and
the exact INR figure before any USDT moves — the number cannot change after
you commit.

**3. Real accounts, real receipts.** A platform with nothing to hide gives
you a login, an order history, an OTP-verified email trail, and a receipt
for every completed payout with the on-chain TXID on it. Anonymous
one-chat-and-gone dealing is how sellers end up with
[no evidence when something goes wrong](/learn/sell-usdt-safely-avoid-scams).

**4. Clean INR, stated and explained.** The money paid to you should come
from the platform's own documented float — not from anonymous third parties.
Why that protects your bank account from freezes is its own topic:
[sell USDT, get clean funds](/learn/sell-usdt-clean-funds), and the desk's
[clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee) spells out the policy.

**5. Indian rails, Indian hours.** UPI, IMPS, CDM and cheque payouts, rupee
amounts in lakh and crore where they belong, and support that answers in
your working day. A desk built for India handles the details that matter
here — bank cut-off times, IFSC validation, payout references — instead of
treating India as an afterthought.

## The ten-minute verification test

You do not have to trust this page. Test any platform — including this one —
the same way:

1. **Start small.** Open an order for a small amount you can afford to
   ignore. A [live order](/sell) costs nothing to create.
2. **Check the lock.** Does the order state the exact rate and INR figure
   before you send anything? Save it.
3. **Send and watch.** Does the platform find your transfer on-chain by
   itself, and show you the real transaction ID? Cross-check that TXID on a
   public blockchain explorer — it should be your transfer, to the address
   you were given.
4. **Time the payout.** Did INR arrive in your bank, from a consistent
   source, at the promised rate, with a receipt?
5. **Only then scale up.** A platform that passes with ₹2,000 will pass with
   ₹2,00,000 — and now you have evidence, not adjectives.

## Why India-specific verification matters

Global marketplaces verify their *users*; they cannot verify the *rupees*
those users pay with. In India that gap is exactly where sellers get hurt —
a frozen account because a stranger's payment traced back to a fraud
complaint. A desk that is the counterparty itself, verifies deposits
on-chain, and pays from its own clean float closes that gap. That
combination — not a badge on a homepage — is what a verified Indian platform
actually is.
