The single biggest fear for anyone selling USDT in India is not the rate and
not the blockchain — it is waking up to find their bank account frozen. The
fear is justified, because it happens constantly on random P2P trades. Here
is exactly why it happens, and the only reliable way to prevent it.

## Why accounts get frozen

When a scam victim anywhere in India reports a fraud, the police trace where
the victim's money went. Every account in that chain — including account
number seven, which received a transfer three hops after the scam — can be
flagged, and banks respond by freezing or putting a lien on the flagged
account. The freeze is not about **you**. It is about **the money that was
sent to you**.

Now look at a typical P2P trade: you sell USDT to a stranger, and the
stranger pays you from an account you know nothing about. If that money —
or the money behind it — came from a scam, a betting app, or a mule
account, the trace eventually reaches your account. You did nothing wrong,
but your salary account is now stuck while an investigation crawls forward,
sometimes for months.

## The uncomfortable truth about "high rate" buyers

Dirty money buys crypto at above-market rates precisely **because** clean
sellers won't touch it knowingly. That premium in a stranger's offer is
often the price of the risk being quietly transferred to you. This is why
"cheapest" and "safest" pull in opposite directions — a theme covered in
detail in our [best-rate guide](/learn/sell-usdt-best-rate-india).

## The only real prevention: control the source of the INR

You cannot vet a stranger's bank account. What you can do is sell to a desk
that controls — and can explain — where every rupee it pays out comes from.
On this desk, payouts come only from verified clean sources:

- Stock-market and mutual-fund settlement withdrawals — money with a complete paper trail
- Physical cash and CDM deposits — clean at the moment they enter the banking system
- Credit-card and payment-gateway settlements from regular commerce

No gaming or betting money, no third-party transfers from strangers, nothing
from anyone the desk cannot identify. That sourcing policy is written down in
the [AML policy](/legal/aml), and the promise built on top of it is the
[100% clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee): because there is no dirty sender
anywhere in the chain, there is nothing for a fraud trace to reach.

## If your account is already frozen from a past trade

This desk cannot unfreeze an account another trade froze, but the standard
path is: get the freeze reference from your bank, find which police
cyber-cell raised the request (your bank must tell you), respond formally
with proof of your side of the trade — the on-chain transaction hash, the
chat, the rate agreement — and follow up in writing. A lawyer speeds this up
substantially. Keep records of **every** crypto sale; on this desk your
[order history](/my) with transaction hashes and proof exists automatically.

## The checklist that keeps your account safe

- Never accept INR from an account you cannot identify
- Payout must go to a bank account in your own name (third-party payouts are refused here — see the [terms](/legal/terms))
- Keep the transaction hash and order record of every sale
- Sell against a written clean-funds commitment, not a verbal "don't worry bro"
