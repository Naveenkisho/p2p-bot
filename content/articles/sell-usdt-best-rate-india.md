Everyone selling USDT wants the same thing: the most rupees per dollar. But
"best rate" ads are where this market hides its worst deals. Here is how
pricing actually works, so you can compare offers like someone who does this
daily.

## The three prices hiding inside one "rate"

When someone quotes you a USDT rate, three separate things are bundled in:

- **The headline rate** — the number in the ad.
- **Deductions** — "processing fees", "TDS handling", percentage cuts, or a worse rate applied after you have already sent your USDT.
- **Risk cost** — the one nobody prices in: whether the INR you receive is clean, and whether the buyer pays at all.

A rate that is ₹0.50 higher but comes with a 1% "fee" is worse than the
honest number. A rate ₹2 higher from an unknown Telegram buyer who pays you
from a scam-money account is the most expensive trade you will ever make —
frozen funds can take months to release, if they release at all. Our
[bank-freeze guide](/learn/avoid-bank-freeze-when-selling-usdt) explains how
that happens.

## What a realistic USDT-INR rate looks like

USDT trades in India at a premium over the USD-INR interbank rate — the
premium moves with demand, typically a few percent. Any offer dramatically
above the going market is funded by something: fees you'll discover later,
money that isn't clean, or a buyer who doesn't intend to pay. There is no
magic buyer paying far over market out of generosity. If you see the market
premium quoted on several serious desks and one stranger offers well above
it, the outlier is the trap.

## Comparing offers: a 60-second method

- Ask for the **net** amount that will hit your bank for your exact USDT amount. Not the rate — the final rupee figure.
- Ask **when** it arrives. A better rate paid "within 48 hours" is a loan you're giving the buyer, priced free.
- Ask **where the INR comes from**. A serious desk can answer in one sentence; read our [clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee) to see what a real answer looks like.
- Check the rate is **locked at order time** in writing, on a page you can revisit — not in a chat message that can be deleted.

Run every "amazing rate" you find through those four questions and the cheap

## The rate that matters is the one in writing

A final habit separates sellers who consistently get good value from those
who chase numbers: they compare quotes, not advertisements. A rate on a
banner is marketing; a rate written into an order you can screenshot is a
commitment. When you compare desks, open a real quote at each for the same
amount and method, note the exact INR figure each one commits to, and
factor the things this guide covered — fees, TDS, waiting time, and whose
money pays you. The best rate on paper that arrives late, taxed, or from a
stranger's account is not the best rate. The honest way to win this game
is boring: a locked quote, clean funds, and a payout that lands while
you're still watching the page.
