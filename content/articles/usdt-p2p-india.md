"P2P" — peer to peer — is the most searched way to trade USDT in India,
and the most misunderstood. The promise is simple: skip the order book,
trade directly with another person, keep more of the rate. The part the
offer listings never explain is what you take on in exchange. Here is how
P2P USDT trading in India actually works, told straight.

## How marketplace P2P works

On a P2P marketplace you post or accept an offer. A buyer agrees to your
price, the platform locks your USDT in escrow, the buyer transfers rupees
directly to your bank account, you confirm the money arrived, and escrow
releases the coins. Every large exchange runs a section like this, and the
mechanics are genuinely clever: the escrow means nobody can walk away with
your crypto without paying.

## What escrow protects — and what it cannot

Escrow protects the **crypto leg** completely. It does nothing for the
**rupee leg**, because the platform never touches the money — it moves
from a stranger's bank account straight into yours. Escrow cannot tell you
whether those rupees were the buyer's own savings or money lifted from a
phishing victim an hour earlier. If it was stolen, the victim's complaint
eventually freezes every account the money passed through — including the
honest seller's. That mechanism, and how to defend against it, is the
subject of
[how sellers get bank accounts frozen](/learn/avoid-bank-freeze-when-selling-usdt).

Disputes are the second gap. When a buyer claims "I paid" and you say
nothing arrived, the marketplace referees with screenshots and bank
statements. You can win those — but you are spending hours to do it, and
the coins sit locked in escrow the whole time.

## Why P2P rates look better

Because you are being paid for four risks the listing never itemises:
payment risk (whose money is it), dispute risk (your time and locked
coins), counterparty risk (the buyer vanishing mid-trade), and account
risk (your bank noticing a parade of strangers' transfers and asking
questions). Experienced traders price those in and still find P2P
worthwhile at times; casual sellers usually discover them one incident at
a time. The honest all-in comparison across exchange, P2P and OTC routes
is in [convert USDT to INR](/learn/convert-usdt-to-inr).

## The direct alternative: peer-to-desk

There is a second meaning of "trading person to person": selling to one
known counterparty instead of an open crowd — an OTC desk. On this site
the desk itself is the buyer for every order: the rate is written into
your order before you send, the transfer is verified automatically on the
public blockchain, and the payout comes from the desk's own operating
float by UPI or IMPS, with a receipt carrying the transaction ID. Because
no stranger's money ever touches your account, the frozen-account trap is
removed at the source — that is the desk's
[clean-funds guarantee](/guarantee), and it exists to protect the seller.
The fuller comparison with marketplace trading is in
[the Binance P2P alternative guide](/learn/binance-p2p-alternative-india).

## If you still trade on an open marketplace

Some sellers will, and that is a legitimate choice — made safer with
rules:

1. **Match the name.** Accept payment only from a bank account in the
   buyer's own verified name. Third-party payments — "my friend will pay"
   — are the single biggest red flag in Indian P2P.
2. **Confirm in your bank, not in a screenshot.** Release escrow only
   after the money shows in your actual account or passbook app.
3. **Start small with every new counterparty**, whatever their completion
   rate says.
4. **Keep records** — order ID, transaction hash, rate, payer name — for
   every single trade. More field-tested rules are in
   [how to sell USDT safely](/learn/sell-usdt-safely-avoid-scams).

## The short version

P2P in India is two different games. Open-marketplace P2P offers headline
rates and hands you the payment risk; peer-to-desk trading gives you one
accountable counterparty, a locked rate and clean funds. Know which game
you are playing before the rupees land. If you want to see the direct
version in action, the [live rate is on the sell page](/sell) — one
counterparty, one written quote, one verified payment.
